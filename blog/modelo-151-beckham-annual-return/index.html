Modelo 151 explained: your annual return under the Beckham regime

JL

Javier López Founder · holds the Beckham regime · not a lawyer

Published: 14 September 2026 · 5 min read
Modelo 151annual returndeadlines

The Beckham Law has two forms. Modelo 149 is the one everyone talks about: the election, with its 6-month deadline. Modelo 151 is the one you live with: the annual income tax return you file every year you are in the regime. This article explains what goes into it and what does not.

This article is general information, not tax advice.

What Modelo 151 is

Modelo 151 is the special income tax return for people taxed under the regime of article 93 of the Spanish income tax law. It replaces the ordinary Modelo 100 for as long as the regime applies. The current version was approved, together with Modelo 149, by Order HFP/1338/2023.

The regime covers the tax year in which you become Spanish tax resident and the following five, so a full regime means six Modelo 151 returns.

Who files it and when

  • Everyone in the regime, every year. The Tax Agency states that taxpayers who opt for the regime are obliged to file Modelo 151. There is no income threshold below which you are excused.
  • Each person separately. There is no joint filing. A spouse or child who joined the regime files their own Modelo 151.
  • In the general income tax period. It is filed where and when the ordinary return is filed: normally from early April to 30 June of the following year. Dates change slightly each year, so check the Tax Agency’s calendar.
  • Electronically, with a digital certificate or Cl@ve.

What goes in

  • All your employment income earned while in the regime: salary, bonuses, benefits in kind and equity, wherever the work was done. It is taxed at 24% up to €600,000 and 47% above. That €600,000 line applies to your whole general base, not just the salary.
  • Spanish-source savings income: interest from Spanish banks, dividends from Spanish companies and gains on Spanish assets, taxed on the savings scale.
  • Spanish property: rental income from a property in Spain, taxed on the gross amount, and the imputed income for a Spanish property that is not rented. Whether imputed income also applies to your main home is disputed between the Tax Agency’s administrative tribunal and the courts; confirm how to handle it with the professional.
  • Withholding already deducted, which is credited against the result.

What stays out

  • Foreign non-employment income: in general, dividends, interest, gains, rentals and private pensions from outside Spain are not declared on Modelo 151.
  • Allowances and most deductions: the personal and family allowances, joint filing, the pension-plan reduction and the regional deductions all disappear.
  • Losses: income is taxed without offsetting one item against another, so a loss does not reduce a gain.

What remains available is limited: the deduction for donations and a deduction for double taxation on employment income earned abroad, with a cap.

An illustrative case

The numbers below are round and invented, for illustration only.

Priya has been in the regime since last year. Her Spanish salary is €100,000, she has a savings account at a Spanish bank, and she rents out her flat in Bristol.

  • Salary: it goes on Modelo 151 at 24%. Our savings calculator puts the Beckham tax on €100,000 at €24,000.
  • Spanish bank interest: it goes in, on the savings scale.
  • The Bristol rent: foreign rental income, in general not declared in Spain under the regime. The UK may tax it.
  • Withholding: her employer only started withholding at 24% in March, when she handed over the Tax Agency’s certificate; January and February were withheld on the general scale. The difference is settled on Modelo 151.

Her return is simple because her income is simple. Add a Spanish rental property, Spanish shares, a second payer or a year with a large vest, and it is not.

When the regime ends

  • After the sixth tax year the regime ends on its own, with no filing. From the seventh you file Modelo 100 on worldwide income, report foreign assets on Modelo 720 when they cross the thresholds, and pay wealth tax on worldwide assets where your region charges it.
  • If you renounce (a Modelo 149 filed in November or December), you leave the regime from the following year, for good.
  • If you breach a requirement, you are excluded from that tax year, and have one month to report it with Modelo 149.
  • If you leave Spain, you report the end of the posting with Modelo 149 within one month.

The years before the exit are the ones to plan, see what happens when the Beckham Law ends.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to file Modelo 151 if my income was low? +

Yes. The Spanish Tax Agency states that taxpayers who opt for the regime are obliged to file Modelo 151. The income thresholds that exempt some people from filing Modelo 100 do not apply to it.

Can I file the normal Modelo 100 instead? +

No. While you are in the regime your return is Modelo 151. Filing Modelo 100 instead can be read as inconsistent with the regime, so do not do it.

When is Modelo 151 due? +

In the same period as the general income tax return, which normally runs from early April to 30 June of the year after the tax year. It is filed electronically. Check each year's dates on the Tax Agency's website.

What do I file after the regime ends? +

From the first tax year outside the regime you file Modelo 100, as an ordinary resident taxed on worldwide income. The same happens from the year after a renunciation, and from the year of an exclusion.
