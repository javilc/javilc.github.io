The Beckham Law 6-month deadline: how it works and what to do if you're late

JL

Javier López Founder · holds the Beckham regime · not a lawyer

Published: 1 September 2026 · Updated: 6 September 2026 · 8 min read
Form 149deadlinesrequirements

If you learn one thing about Spain’s special expat regime, the “Beckham Law”, make it this: you have 6 months from your Social Security registration to apply, and there are no second chances. It is by far the number-one reason perfectly eligible people end up paying up to 47–50% income tax instead of 24%.

The rule comes from article 93 of the Personal Income Tax Act and its implementing regulation. The regime is an option: you have to elect it, in writing, with a specific form, inside a specific window. Miss the window and the option simply no longer exists for that relocation, no matter how clearly you meet every other requirement.

How the clock actually starts

You apply with Form 149 (Modelo 149) to the Spanish tax agency (AEAT). The 6-month window is counted from the date that evidences the start of your work in Spain, which depends on your route:

Your route into the regimeThe date the 6 months count from
Employment contract with a Spanish employerYour registration with Spanish Social Security (alta)
Employer-ordered relocation, keeping home-country coverageThe date on the certificate of coverage (A1 or equivalent) that lets you stay in your home system
International remote work for a foreign employer (digital nomad route)Your Spanish Social Security registration, or the certificate of coverage if your employer’s country has a treaty with Spain
Company directorThe registration or document evidencing the start of the directorship

Note what does not start the clock: not your flight date, not your town-hall registration (empadronamiento), not your contract signature, not getting your NIE, not the day your visa is granted. This misunderstanding is the most common cause of late applications, in both directions. People count from arrival and think they are on time when they are not, or give up believing they are late when weeks remain.

Write the trigger date down the day it happens. It is the single most important date of your relocation. If you want the exact due date for your case, the Modelo 149 deadline calculator works it out in your browser.

What the deadline is actually worth

The regime taxes employment income at a flat 24% (up to €600,000; 47% on the excess) instead of the progressive scale, but takes away the personal allowance and almost every deduction. The point of the table is simpler than the numbers: the difference between filing in month 5 and filing in month 7 is not a fine or a delay. It is the whole amount, for up to six years.

A month-by-month plan

Before you register. Confirm your route (contract, relocation letter, remote work, directorship) and that you have not been a Spanish tax resident in any of the previous five tax years. Fix the order of the steps if your plan involves a company or self-employment, see why freelancers don’t qualify by default.

Week 1 after registration. Note the exact registration date. Gather the supporting documents: passport or ID, NIE, employment contract or relocation letter, Social Security registration or certificate of coverage, and proof of your previous tax residence if you have it (tax-residence certificates take time, request them now).

Weeks 2–6. File Form 149 electronically. You need a digital certificate or Cl@ve to file yourself, or a representative with power of attorney. There is no advantage in waiting: nothing in the procedure improves with time, and everything that can go wrong gets worse.

Months 2–3. AEAT normally answers within one to two months, issuing a certificate that you are covered by the regime. Give a copy to your employer so payroll can switch to the non-resident withholding scheme (Modelo 216) and apply 24% from then on; excess tax withheld earlier is settled in your first annual return.

Months 4–6. If you have not filed yet, you are in the danger zone. Filing is still perfectly possible, but any information request from AEAT that asks for a missing document now lands close to the wall. Treat it as urgent.

The following year. Your annual return under the regime is Form 151 (Modelo 151), not the standard Modelo 100, filed in the normal income-tax campaign. Put it in the calendar for every year the regime lasts.

The mistakes that kill the most applications

  1. “I’ll sort it out once I’m settled.” Between the move, schools and paperwork, 6 months evaporate. You can file from day one, there is zero advantage in waiting.
  2. Counting from the wrong date. Arrival, contract signature and empadronamiento are all irrelevant. Only the registration or certificate of coverage starts the clock.
  3. Registering as self-employed “in the meantime”. An autónomo registration before or on arrival can compromise your route into the regime. If your plan includes freelancing, the order of the steps matters enormously.
  4. Assuming the visa is the tax election. The digital nomad visa is granted by the immigration authority and says nothing to AEAT. Two procedures, two authorities, two deadlines.
  5. “HR said they would handle it.” Employers register you with Social Security and run payroll; very few file Form 149 for you, and the filing is in your name. Ask, in writing, who is doing it and when.
  6. Waiting for paperwork that isn’t a prerequisite. You do not need to wait for a TIE card, a rental contract or a Spanish bank account to file. If you cannot get a digital certificate quickly, a representative can file for you.

What if the deadline has already passed?

Honest answer: options are limited. The deadline is what lawyers call preclusive: once it passes, the right lapses, and the tax office applies it strictly and rejects late filings without weighing how good the rest of the case was. Before writing anything off, verify two things:

  • What is your real start date? We have seen cases where the effective Social Security registration was later than the taxpayer believed, because the employer registered late, or because the coverage certificate was issued after arrival, and there was still time.
  • Is there a second window? If you have not yet become a Spanish tax resident (for example, you arrived in the second half of the year and will not cross 183 days), restructuring the relocation can in some scenarios open a fresh opportunity. This is delicate territory that needs professional case-by-case analysis; it is not a trick, and it is not always available.

If neither applies, a late filing will be rejected. Appeals exist, a reconsideration request to AEAT, then the regional economic-administrative tribunal, then the courts, but they succeed only on narrow grounds such as a genuine dispute over which date started the clock. They are not a way to “get an extension”.

The fallback is planning your regular income tax well: it won’t be 24%, but flexible-remuneration schemes, pension contributions, income timing and, where relevant, the exemption for work performed abroad all cut the bill. Losing the regime is expensive; losing it and not planning is worse.

The other clocks you inherit once you are in

The 6-month deadline is the famous one, but the regime comes with a calendar of its own:

  • Form 151 every year, in the standard income-tax campaign, for as long as you are in the regime.
  • Renouncing the regime is only possible in November–December of the year before it would take effect, and it is irrevocable, you cannot opt back in later.
  • If you stop meeting a requirement (for example, your employment ends and you start invoicing through a permanent establishment), you must notify AEAT within one month; the regime ends and you cannot re-elect it.
  • Spouse and children under 25 can join the regime, but each of them files their own Form 149 with its own 6-month window counted from their own trigger date.
  • The regime covers the tax year of your move plus the five following ones. Years five and six are when to plan the exit, see what happens when the Beckham Law ends.

Checklist

  • Route into the regime confirmed before registering with Social Security or incorporating anything.
  • Trigger date (registration or certificate of coverage) written down and shared with whoever helps you.
  • Digital certificate or Cl@ve obtained, or a representative appointed.
  • Supporting documents collected; tax-residence certificates from previous countries requested.
  • Form 149 filed within the first weeks, not the last.
  • AEAT certificate forwarded to your employer for 24% withholding.
  • Form 151 in the calendar for every year of the regime.

The golden rule

File Form 149 as early as possible, ideally within the first weeks after registration. The tax office’s confirmation gives you certainty to set your payroll withholding at 24% from the start, and no surprises at your first tax return. If your case has an edge to it, your own company, a directorship, a family relocating with you, US citizenship, get it reviewed before you file, not after a rejection. Frequently asked questions

When does the 6-month Beckham Law deadline start counting? +

From the date of your registration with Spanish Social Security (or the date on the paperwork that lets you keep your home-country coverage, such as an A1 certificate). It does not count from your arrival in Spain or from signing your contract.

Can I apply for the Beckham Law after the deadline? +

The law provides no extensions and the tax office systematically rejects late filings. Administrative and court appeals exist but succeed only on narrow grounds, the safe route is filing Form 149 on time.

What if my employer registered me with Social Security late? +

The clock runs from the effective registration date, not your first day of work. A late registration can actually buy you deadline time, but it creates other employment-law risks, worth reviewing case by case.

Does applying for the digital nomad visa stop the 6-month clock? +

No. The visa is an immigration procedure before a different authority. The tax election is a separate filing (Form 149) with its own 6-month window counted from your Social Security registration or certificate of coverage. Holding the visa and filing late still means losing the regime.

How soon can I file Form 149? +

From the day your Social Security registration (or certificate of coverage) exists. There is no minimum waiting period and no advantage in delaying: the earlier you file, the earlier your employer can withhold at 24% and the less risk of a paperwork problem eating your window.
