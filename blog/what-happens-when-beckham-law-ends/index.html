When the Beckham Law ends: what happens after the 6 years

JL

Javier López Founder · holds the Beckham regime · not a lawyer

Published: 7 July 2026 · 2 min read
planningregime exitForm 720

The Beckham Law is not forever: it covers the year you become a tax resident plus the 5 following tax years. The day it ends, your tax life changes completely, and the difference between preparing two years ahead or discovering it at your first ordinary tax return can be worth tens of thousands of euros.

What exactly changes when you exit

ItemUnder BeckhamAfter (ordinary income tax)
Employment income24% flat (47% > €600,000)Progressive scale, up to ~47%
Foreign income (dividends, gains, rent)Generally not taxed in SpainWorldwide taxation
Foreign assetsNo Form 720Form 720 mandatory
Wealth / large-fortunes taxSpanish assets onlyWorldwide assets
Personal allowances and deductionsNot availableAvailable

The sharpest change is usually not the salary rate but worldwide taxation: brokerage accounts, dividends from your old employer, the rented-out flat back home, funds… everything enters the Spanish return. And with it, Form 720 (the informational return on foreign assets) with its €50,000 thresholds per asset class.

The final-2-years window

The last two years of the regime are when the decisions get made, because you’re still playing under Beckham rules:

  • Realise foreign gains. Latent gains on foreign-source shares or funds that are untaxed in Spain under the regime can be crystallised before the switch; afterwards they’d cost up to ~30%. (Always check the source country’s rule.)
  • Pull deferred pay forward. Deferred bonuses, accelerable vests or option exercises can be concentrated into the 24% years.
  • Reorganise the portfolio. Investment structures that are neutral for a non-resident can be tax-hostile for an ordinary resident (non-harmonised funds, some non-EU ETFs, foreign holding entities). Restructuring is cheaper before.
  • Prepare Form 720. Inventorying accounts, securities and property in advance avoids filing late a return whose penalty history is infamous.

Stay or leave?

When the regime ends there are three paths:

  1. Stay as an ordinary resident. The majority case for people with a life built here. The key is entering the general system with your assets already in order.
  2. Leave Spain. Some high-mobility profiles relocate to another jurisdiction. Watch the exit tax (on latent gains in significant shareholdings, applicable once you’ve been resident 10 of the last 15 years, Beckham years count for this) and the timing of a mid-year departure.
  3. Waive early. In specific cases (an income drop, strong family deductions) the general regime can beat Beckham. The waiver is filed in November–December and is irrevocable.

The most expensive mistake: doing nothing

Every year we see the same case: someone whose regime ended in December discovers in May that their US dividends are now taxable, that three accounts and a flat must go on Form 720, and that they sold shares in January, one month too late, paying 30% on something that would have been 0%. All avoidable with an exit plan.

Frequently asked questions

Can the Beckham Law be extended beyond 6 years? +

No. The regime applies in the tax year of the move and the five following years, with no extension possible. When it ends, you automatically become an ordinary resident under the general income tax.

Do I have to file Form 720 while under the Beckham Law? +

No. While under the regime there is no obligation to report foreign assets (Form 720). The obligation starts in your first year as an ordinary resident if you exceed the €50,000 thresholds per asset class.

Can I opt out of the Beckham Law early? +

Yes, by express waiver filed in November–December with effect for the following year. It can make sense in specific cases (lower income, significant family deductions), but the waiver is irrevocable: you can never return to the regime.
